Utility Bill Decoder

1. The challenge

Most people find their electricity bill hard to follow. It mixes tariff slabs, taxes, fuel adjustments and many
line items, and each provider lays it out differently. Your challenge is to build an AI-powered assistant that
reads a bill, pulls out its details, and explains them in plain language.

The input is an electricity bill from K-Electric, LESCO or IESCO, as a scanned image, screenshot or photo.
Layouts vary: some bills are tilted or partly in Urdu, and names, addresses and ID numbers are covered
with grey boxes.

This guide explains the data, output formats, submission requirements and scoring.

5.2 General extraction rules

These rules apply to every field in the JSON.

     Extract values as printed. Do not recalculate, round, or correct bill amounts.
     Amounts are numbers in PKR. Remove currency labels and commas: Rs. 19,964 becomes 19964.
     Credits and subsidies are negative. For example, 38209CR becomes -38209. Record a subsidy as a

         deduction.

     Use the required date formats. 22nd Sep 2026 becomes 2026-09-22; MAR 2026 becomes
         2026-03.

     Use null for unavailable values: not printed, blank, covered by a grey box, or unreadable. A
         correct null is scored as correct. Do not guess. Use [] for an empty list.

     Exclude personal identifiers. Do not return names, addresses, CNIC, account numbers, reference
         numbers, consumer numbers, or meter numbers.

6. Level 2: Answer customer questions

In Level 2, your solution answers customer questions about each bill in plain English, using only
information on the bill. Your solution must generate one text answer for every row in level2.csv. All 10
test bills have the same 12 questions, so that is 120 answers in total.

6.1 Answer rules

Follow these rules for every answer.

     Get the numbers right. Averages, differences, counts, percentages, and estimates are checked
         against the bill. Using code for calculations is recommended.

     Ground each answer in the bill. Do not use outside tariff or rate information. If the bill does not
         show a needed value, say so instead of guessing.

     Label estimates. Say that a value is an estimate and explain how you calculated it.
     Explain ambiguous interpretations. If a question has more than one reasonable meaning, state the

         meaning you used or answer both.
     Exclude personal identifiers.
     Answer in English. Write every answer in English, even when parts of the bill are in Urdu.
